# Practice: SQL Aggregations and `GROUP BY`

Try each question using SQL. The sample `employees` and `sales` tables are created in `examples.ipynb`.

1. Count all employees in the employees table.
2. Count employees whose salary is not NULL.
3. Find the total of all employee salaries.
4. Calculate the average employee salary.
5. Find the minimum and maximum employee salary in one query.
6. Count employees in each department.
7. Calculate the average salary for each department.
8. Calculate total payroll for each department.
9. Show each department and its highest salary.
10. Count employees by department and role.
11. Calculate average salary by department and role.
12. Show departments with more than three employees.
13. Show departments whose average salary is greater than 50,000.
14. Find departments with total payroll above 150,000.
15. Count employees in each location.
16. Show departments ordered by employee count from highest to lowest.
17. Show the three departments with the largest payroll.
18. Count distinct roles in the employees table.
19. Count distinct roles in each department.
20. Find the average salary for employees earning at least 30,000.
21. Find each department's average salary after excluding salaries below 30,000.
22. Count employees in each department whose salary is at least 60,000.
23. Use conditional aggregation to count employees earning at least 60,000 in each department.
24. Show the number of employees and average salary for each department, sorted by average salary descending.
25. Show each department's lowest and highest salary.
26. Find the number of sales rows for each product.
27. Calculate total quantity sold for each product.
28. Calculate revenue per product using quantity multiplied by unit_price.
29. Show total sales revenue by region.
30. Show regions with total revenue greater than 1,000.
31. Count unique customers represented in the sales table.
32. Find average unit price by product.
33. Show total revenue by product, highest first, and break ties by product name ascending.
34. Explain the difference between WHERE and HAVING with an example.
35. Explain the difference between COUNT(*) and COUNT(column), including how NULL affects each.

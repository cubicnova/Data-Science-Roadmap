# Practice: SQL CTEs

Try these questions using SQL. Some questions introduce `sales`, `customers`, or `orders` tables as stated.

1. Create a CTE named high_earners that returns employees earning at least 60,000.
2. Use a CTE to calculate average salary by department.
3. Use a CTE to return departments whose average salary exceeds 60,000.
4. Create a CTE that counts employees in each department and display department names.
5. Use a CTE to calculate total payroll by department.
6. Create two CTEs: one for department averages and one for departments with average salary above 60,000.
7. Use a CTE to find employees earning more than the overall average salary.
8. Use a CTE to find employees earning more than their department's average salary.
9. Use a CTE to count orders per customer and preserve customers with zero orders.
10. Use a CTE to calculate each customer's total order amount.
11. Use a CTE to find customers whose total order amount exceeds 500.
12. Use a CTE to calculate total revenue by product from quantity multiplied by unit_price.
13. Use a CTE and a join to show department name and employee count.
14. Use two CTEs to find departments with more than two employees and then display their names.
15. Create a CTE with a filtered subset of employees and query that CTE in the final SELECT.
16. Use a CTE to identify the highest salary in each department.
17. Use a CTE to find employees whose salary equals the maximum salary in their department.
18. Use a CTE to calculate average order amount by order status.
19. Use a CTE to find order statuses whose total amount exceeds 500.
20. Use a recursive CTE to generate integers from 1 through 10.
21. Use a recursive CTE to generate the first 10 multiples of 3.
22. Explain the anchor and recursive parts of a recursive CTE.
23. Explain the difference between a CTE and a derived table.
24. Explain the difference between a CTE and a temporary table.
25. Use a CTE to calculate monthly revenue, then calculate a running total.
26. Use a CTE to find the top three employees by salary.
27. Use a CTE to rank employees by salary within each department.
28. Use a CTE to identify departments that have no employees.
29. Use a CTE to find customers who have placed at least one completed order.
30. Use a CTE to find customers who have never placed an order.
31. Use a CTE to calculate department payroll and compare it with average department payroll.
32. Use a CTE to remove duplicate values from a list of city names.
33. Describe two common CTE syntax mistakes and how to correct them.
34. Explain why a CTE is not necessarily faster than an equivalent subquery.
35. Write a query with two CTEs to find employees above their department average, ordered by salary descending.
